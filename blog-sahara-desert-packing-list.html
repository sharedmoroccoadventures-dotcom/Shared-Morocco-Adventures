What to Pack for a Sahara Desert Group Tour

The desert is hot by day and cold by night — pack for both, and pack lighter than you think you need to.

The single most common surprise for first-time desert visitors is the temperature swing: a Sahara afternoon can hit 30°C while the same night drops to 5-10°C, sometimes colder in winter. Add sand that gets into everything and a shared 4x4 or minivan with limited luggage space, and packing smart matters more here than almost anywhere else on a Morocco trip.

Clothing: Layers Are Everything

Skip the single heavy jacket and pack layers instead — a moisture-wicking t-shirt, a light long-sleeve for sun protection, a fleece or light sweater, and a proper warm layer (down jacket or heavy fleece) for the camp at night. Loose, breathable trousers work better than shorts, both for sun protection and cultural respect in villages along the route. A pair of comfortable closed shoes handles the dune walk to camp; sandals are fine for lounging at camp itself but useless once you're on actual sand dunes. Pack a warm hat and gloves if you're traveling November through February — desert camp nights genuinely get cold enough to need them.

Sand and Sun Protection

A headscarf or shemagh (sold cheaply throughout Marrakech and Merzouga, roughly €3-8) is the single most useful item you'll pack — it blocks sun, wind, and blowing sand during the camel trek and dune walks. Wraparound sunglasses beat regular ones since they keep sand out of your eyes on windy afternoons. High-SPF sunscreen and lip balm with SPF are essential; the desert sun is stronger than it feels thanks to the dry air and reflection off the sand.

💡

Quick Tip

Pack a dry bag or large ziplock for your phone and camera during the camel trek — sand kicked up by the camels ahead of you gets everywhere, and a bit of dust protection saves your electronics.

Practical Essentials

Desert camps generally don't have reliable power outlets, so bring a fully charged power bank — you'll want your phone for photos of the sunset and stars without worrying about battery. Pack a headlamp or small torch; camps are lit but the walk between tents and the dune-top stargazing spot is genuinely dark. Bring small cash in dirhams for tips to your camel handlers and camp staff — €3-5 per person per activity is a reasonable, appreciated amount, and camps rarely have card facilities. A refillable water bottle is worth it too, since most shared tours provide bottled water refill stops along the route.

What to Leave Behind

Leave the rolling suitcase at your riad in Marrakech or Fes if your tour offers luggage storage — a soft duffel or backpack is far easier to load into a shared minivan and carry the short stretch to camp. Skip heavy hardcover books, valuables you don't need, and anything white (sand and dust make it a losing battle). Most shared desert tours already include blankets at the camp, so an extra sleeping bag is rarely necessary unless you run especially cold — check with your tour operator before hauling one along.

A Simple Checklist

Layered clothing (base layer, warm layer, jacket), closed shoes, headscarf, sunglasses, sunscreen and lip balm, power bank, headlamp, cash for tips, refillable water bottle, and a soft bag rather than a hard suitcase. Pack it all into one manageable bag and you'll be glad you did the moment you're loading it onto a camel.
