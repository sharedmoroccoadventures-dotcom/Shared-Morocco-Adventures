Fes vs Marrakech: Which Medina to Visit First

Two of the world's great living medinas, two very different experiences — here's how to pick if you can only choose one.

3 min readCity GuideBy the Shared Morocco Adventures team

Ask ten travelers whether to visit Fes or Marrakech first and you'll get ten different answers, usually delivered with strong opinions. Both cities have UNESCO-listed medinas, both will disorient you in the best way, and both are worth seeing eventually. But if your budget or your itinerary only stretches to one on this trip, the choice comes down to what kind of experience you actually want.

Atmosphere: Lively vs Traditional

Marrakech is the extrovert. Jemaa el-Fnaa fills with drummers, food stalls, and snake charmers every evening, the souks are dense with shops angled squarely at tourists, and there's a buzz to the place that never really switches off. It's exciting, occasionally overwhelming, and unapologetically commercial in parts.

Fes is the introvert with more to say once you're listening. Its medina, Fes el Bali, is the largest car-free urban area in the world, and it feels genuinely lived-in rather than staged for visitors — you'll pass working tanneries, Quranic schools, and neighborhood bakeries alongside the craft stalls. It's quieter, older in feel, and rewards travelers who like wandering without a fixed plan.

Cost Differences

Marrakech's popularity means prices creep higher, especially for riads, guided tours, and anything near the main square — expect to pay a premium of maybe 15-20% over Fes for comparable accommodation. Fes riads in the medina start from around €15-25 a night for a budget double, sometimes a few euros less than similar Marrakech options. Food and taxis are comparable in both cities, though Marrakech's tourist-facing cafés near Jemaa el-Fnaa will charge noticeably more than the same dish two streets back.

Crowd Levels

Marrakech sees far more foot traffic overall, particularly from short-break European visitors, which means busier souks, more persistent touts, and longer queues at sites like Bahia Palace during peak season (November-March, plus Easter). Fes draws fewer day-trippers and almost no short-break crowds, so even at midday its souks feel calmer — though the flip side is it's also easier to get properly lost, since fewer signs cater to tourists.

💡

Quick Tip

If you're combining both cities, do Fes first — its slower pace and quieter medina are a gentler introduction, and Marrakech's energy feels like a fun finale rather than an overwhelming start.

Which One Should You Pick?

If you have 4-5 days or less and want the highlight-reel version of Morocco — desert access, mountain day trips, coastal options, and the country's most famous square — Marrakech is the stronger base. If you have a week or more, care more about history and craftsmanship than nightlife, and don't mind a slower rhythm, start in Fes and work your way south.
