A Backpacker's Guide to the Marrakech Medina

How to find your way through the alleys, know which sights are worth paying for, and eat well without overspending.

The Marrakech medina is a maze on purpose — centuries of narrow lanes built to confuse invaders, and now they do a fine job confusing tourists too. That's part of the charm, but it helps to have a mental map before you dive in. Here's what actually matters if you're travelling on a budget and want to see the medina properly without losing a day to getting lost or hassled.

Get Your Bearings First

Everything radiates out from Jemaa el-Fnaa, the main square. From there, the Koutoubia Mosque's minaret is visible from most rooftops and alleys near the western edge of the medina — use it as your north star when you feel turned around. The souks sit directly north of the square, roughly organized by trade (dyers, leather workers, metalsmiths, carpet sellers), though the organization loosens the deeper you go.

Download an offline map on Maps.me or Google Maps before you arrive — data signal is patchy in the narrowest alleys, and half the lanes aren't named on any map anyway. When in doubt, walking toward noise usually leads back to a main thoroughfare.

Which Sites Are Worth the Entrance Fee

Bahia Palace (around 70 MAD) is genuinely worth it — the carved cedar ceilings and tiled courtyards are some of the best preserved in the city, and it rarely feels overcrowded if you arrive right at opening. The Saadian Tombs (around 70 MAD) are smaller and the queue can be longer than the visit itself, but the marble work is striking and it's a quick stop if you're already nearby.

Skip the Photography Museum and most of the smaller "museums" that touts will steer you toward near the souks — they charge similar entrance fees for a fraction of the substance. The Marrakech Museum is pleasant but skippable if your budget or time is tight. Le Jardin Secret is a nicer, quieter garden alternative to the more famous (and pricier) Majorelle Garden if you want greenery without the crowds.

💡

Quick Tip

Visit Bahia Palace right when it opens at 9am — you'll get soft morning light for photos and beat the tour bus crowds that arrive by mid-morning.

Where Budget Travelers Actually Eat

Skip the tourist-menu restaurants ringing Jemaa el-Fnaa itself — prices jump the moment you sit at a table facing the square. Instead, walk two minutes into any side street and look for where locals are eating: a bowl of harira soup with bread costs 10-15 MAD, a plate of grilled brochettes from a stall runs 20-30 MAD, and a full tagine at a no-frills medina restaurant is typically 35-60 MAD. The food stalls that set up in Jemaa el-Fnaa itself each evening (stalls 1-100-ish, numbered) are an experience worth having once, but confirm prices before you order — ask, point at the menu board, and don't let anyone bring extras to your table without asking first.

Avoiding Hassle: Touts and Fake Guides

The single most common scam in the medina is the "helpful local" who insists a street is closed or a site is shut, then offers to guide you somewhere else for a fee. Sites are almost never actually closed. A polite but firm "la, shukran" (no, thank you) and continuing to walk works better than engaging in conversation. Official guides wear a badge — if someone offers to guide you for free, they are working for commission from a shop, not out of kindness.

This is honestly where travelling with a small group pays for itself: a shared group tour comes with a local guide who already knows which alleys lead where, so you spend your energy actually enjoying the medina instead of negotiating your way out of unwanted "help." It's also simply more fun to wander the souks with a few other travelers than to constantly be on guard alone.

How Long to Spend Here

Two full days is enough to see the highlights properly without rushing — one for the souks and palaces, one for a slower wander plus a hammam visit or day trip out to the Agafay desert or Ourika Valley. If you're short on time, a guided half-day walking tour is the most efficient way to see the essentials without wasting hours backtracking through the same alley three times.
