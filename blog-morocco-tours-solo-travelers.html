Morocco Tours for Solo Travelers: What to Expect

Solo travelers are usually the largest single group on our departures — here's how booking, rooming, and meeting people actually works.

2 min readSolo TravelBy the Shared Morocco Adventures team

If you're weighing whether to travel Morocco solo or book a group tour, here's the short version: on our departures, solo travelers usually make up the largest single group — more than couples, more than friend groups. Here's exactly how that works in practice, from rooming to cost.

Why Shared Tours Work Well When You're Traveling Alone

Traveling solo in Morocco is genuinely doable and plenty of people do it well, but a few things get harder without company: splitting transport and guide costs, not eating dinner alone every single night, and not being the only person deciding where to stop next for two weeks straight. A shared group tour solves all three at once — you're not paying a solo-traveler markup on transport, you've got built-in dinner company most nights, and the route is already planned by someone who knows it.

How Roommate Matching Actually Works

Our listed prices are based on shared twin or dorm-style rooms. If you book solo, you'll be paired with a same-gender roommate for any shared room or shared desert-camp tent — you won't be matched across genders, and you won't be left to awkwardly ask around at check-in. If you'd rather not share at all, a private room is available on every tour as a paid upgrade, subject to availability — worth asking about at booking if you know in advance you'd prefer your own space.

🎒

Quick Tip

If solo travel plus shared rooms feels like a big first step, book a shorter route first — our 3-Day and 4-Day tours are a low-commitment way to test whether the shared format suits you before booking something longer.

Meeting Other Travelers

Groups typically run 6 to 16 travelers, and solo travelers naturally end up spending the most time together simply because they're not already paired off with someone. Long driving days, shared meals, and desert-camp nights do a lot of the work here — it's common for a group to be swapping contact details by day two and still messaging each other months after the trip ends. If you want more on how to make the most of that side of it, see Group Travel Etiquette: Making Friends on the Road.

Cost vs. Planning the Same Route Solo

This is where the math genuinely favors group tours for solo travelers. A private driver-guide covering a multi-day route is priced per vehicle, not per person — so a solo traveler booking privately pays the full cost of a car and guide alone. On a shared tour, that same cost is split across everyone on board, which is typically a 40-60% saving for a solo traveler over the same route booked privately. Our 4-Day Sahara Desert Group Tour is a good example to check the actual numbers on — it's priced per seat from €324, with the day-by-day route laid out in full.

If You're a Solo Woman Traveler

Everything above applies regardless of gender, but if you want the more specific cultural and safety-focused side of solo travel in Morocco — what to wear, navigating medinas, handling unsolicited "help" in the souks — we've written a dedicated guide: Solo Female Travel in Morocco: A Group Tour Guide.
