How Fixed-Departure Group Tours Actually Work

What "fixed departure" really means, how a group actually comes together, and what happens if a trip doesn't sell out.

"Fixed-departure group tour" gets used a lot in Morocco travel listings, but it's rarely explained clearly — and it's worth understanding before you book, because it changes how your trip actually runs. Here's what the term actually means, how a group comes together, and the honest answer to the question everyone asks: what happens if not enough people sign up.

Fixed Departure vs. Private or Custom Tour

A private or custom tour is built around one traveler or group: you set the dates, you set the pace, and the price is yours alone to cover. A fixed-departure tour runs the other way — the route, the dates, and the price are set in advance, and any traveler can book a seat on that specific departure. You're not building an itinerary from scratch; you're joining one that's already scheduled to run, alongside whoever else has booked the same dates.

That's the trade that makes shared tours affordable: transport, the driver-guide, and the shared accommodation are priced per seat rather than per vehicle, so the cost gets split across everyone traveling that day instead of falling on one traveler or one family.

How a Group Actually Comes Together

Departures are scheduled on a fixed weekly or set-day cadence — check each tour page for the exact day it runs. From there, travelers book individually, in couples, or in small friend groups, and the departure fills up seat by seat as bookings come in. Our departures typically run with 6 to 16 travelers on board, which is enough people for the trip to feel social without turning into a large coach-tour crowd — you'll usually recognize everyone's face by day two.

Quick Tip

If you'd rather know exactly who else is likely to be on your departure before you book, ask when you enquire — we can usually tell you how many seats are already reserved for a given date.

What Happens If a Departure Doesn't Fill Up

This is the question that stops a lot of people from booking a shared tour, so here's the straight answer: our departures run on their scheduled date regardless of how many seats are booked. Unlike some operators who cancel or reschedule a trip that hasn't hit a minimum headcount, a fixed departure that's confirmed on the calendar goes ahead — even if you end up being one of only a handful of travelers on board that day. The trade-off is the one above: you don't get to move the date to suit you, because the date was set before you booked.

Who Actually Books These Trips

The travelers on a typical departure are a genuine mix — solo travelers in their 20s and 30s looking to meet people, couples who'd rather split the cost and logistics of a private car, and small friend groups who want the route handled for them. Solo travelers are usually the largest single group, since a fixed departure solves the two things that make solo travel harder: finding people to share the cost with, and not having to plan the route alone. If you're weighing a group tour against planning the same route yourself, our 4-Day Sahara Desert Group Tour is a good example to look at — it's fully scheduled, priced per seat, and shows exactly what's included.

The Short Version

Fixed departure means the trip is already scheduled and priced — you're booking a seat on it, not commissioning it. That's what keeps the price down, and it's also why the date on the tour page is the date you're actually signing up for.
