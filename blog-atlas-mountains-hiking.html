Atlas Mountains Hiking for Beginners

From an easy afternoon waterfall walk to North Africa's highest summit, here's how to pick a hike that matches your fitness and time.

3 min readAdventureBy the Shared Morocco Adventures team

The High Atlas mountains rise less than an hour from Marrakech, which makes them one of the easiest adventure add-ons to any Morocco trip — you can be sipping mint tea in a Berber village by mid-morning and back in the medina for dinner. But "Atlas Mountains hiking" covers a huge range of difficulty, from a flat, well-marked riverside path to a multi-day summit trek above 4,000 metres, so it's worth knowing what you're actually signing up for.

Beginner-Friendly Option: Ourika Valley

The Ourika Valley is the easiest way to get a genuine taste of the Atlas without any real hiking experience. Roads wind past terraced fields and Berber villages up to Setti Fatma, where a series of seven waterfalls cascades down the hillside. The walk up to the first two or three falls is a manageable 1-2 hours round trip on a rocky but clear path — sturdy trainers are fine, no technical gear needed. Going further up to all seven falls adds another hour or two and a bit more scrambling, but it's still achievable for most reasonably fit beginners.

This is exactly the kind of day trip that works well as a shared group outing — a fixed departure with a driver and guide costs a fraction of arranging a private car and guide yourself, and it's a nice, sociable way to spend a day outside the city with other travelers.

Bigger Commitment: Toubkal Summit

Jbel Toubkal, at 4,167 metres, is North Africa's highest peak, and reaching the summit is a genuinely different undertaking from a valley walk. Most people do it as a two-day trek: hike roughly 5-6 hours up to the Toubkal refuge (around 3,200m) on day one, sleep there, then make a pre-dawn push of another 4-6 hours to the summit and back down to the refuge or all the way to the trailhead on day two. No technical climbing skill is required outside winter conditions, but it demands solid fitness, proper layers, and ideally a licensed mountain guide, which is legally required in the national park.

💡

Quick Tip

If Toubkal is on your list, spend at least one night at moderate elevation beforehand if you can — even a night in Imlil (around 1,700m) helps your body start adjusting before the refuge night higher up.

What to Wear and Bring

For the Ourika Valley walk: sturdy closed-toe shoes, a light layer, sun protection, and water are enough. For Toubkal, you'll want proper hiking boots with ankle support, a warm insulated layer plus a waterproof shell (temperatures at the refuge drop well below freezing outside summer), gloves and a hat even in warmer months, trekking poles for the scree sections, and a headlamp for the pre-dawn summit push. Refuges provide basic bedding and meals, so you don't need to carry camping gear.

Altitude and Fitness Considerations

The Ourika Valley trails sit at low enough elevation that altitude isn't a factor for most people. Toubkal is a different story — mild altitude effects like headache or breathlessness are common above 3,000m even for fit hikers, and rushing the ascent makes it worse. Building in that first night at the refuge rather than attempting the summit in one long push from Imlil dramatically improves how you'll feel, and gives your body time to acclimatize. If you have any underlying heart or lung conditions, check with a doctor before attempting the summit trek.

Whichever end of the spectrum you're aiming for, going with a group rather than solo is genuinely safer in the mountains — shared costs for a licensed guide, backup in case anyone struggles with the altitude or terrain, and simply more fun company on what can be a long, quiet trail.
