Best Souvenirs to Buy in Morocco (and What to Skip)

The souks are full of genuinely beautiful craftsmanship — and a fair amount aimed squarely at tourists who don't know the difference.

Morocco's souks can feel overwhelming — every stall seems to sell some version of the same lanterns, leather, and textiles. A little context on what's genuinely worth your suitcase space (and what's mass-produced filler) makes shopping far more enjoyable and a lot less like guesswork.

Worth Buying

Argan oil — genuine cosmetic and culinary argan oil from a women's cooperative is one of Morocco's best-value products; buy from a cooperative shop rather than a random souk stall for a better guarantee of authenticity. Ceramics and pottery — Fes and Safi are the traditional centers, known for blue-and-white geometric patterns and tagine pots that are genuinely functional, not just decorative. Berber rugs — vintage and new wool or kilim rugs from the Atlas and Middle Atlas regions, with prices varying hugely by size, age, and knot density; buying from a cooperative or a shop that explains the region and technique tends to be more reliable than a generic souk stall. Leather goods — bags, poufs, and babouche slippers from the Fes and Marrakech tanneries, best inspected for stitching quality and a genuine leather smell rather than a plastic one. Spices and saffron — sold by weight in the souk, a good way to bring home something you'll actually use.

💡

Quick Tip

Real saffron is expensive everywhere — genuine Moroccan saffron rarely sells for a bargain price. If a deal looks too good, it's likely safflower or turmeric dyed to look similar.

Worth Skipping

Mass-produced "handmade" items stamped out in bulk (often identical across ten stalls in a row) rarely justify a premium price. Cheap "argan oil" sold in unmarked bottles at rock-bottom prices is frequently cut with other oils. Fossils and "ancient" trilobites sold at desert stops are almost always modern reproductions, however convincing they look. And oversized rugs bought on impulse are worth a pause — factor in how you'll actually get a large rug home before committing.

Where to Buy

Souks give you the widest selection and the most room to negotiate, but require some confidence and a willingness to walk away — see our full guide to bargaining in Moroccan souks before you start shopping seriously. Women's cooperatives (for argan oil, rugs, and weaving) typically have fixed or near-fixed prices, and buying there supports the artisans more directly than a middleman shop.

Getting It Home

Small items pack easily in checked luggage — wrap ceramics in clothing for padding. Larger rugs and furniture can usually be shipped by the seller for an additional fee; ask for a written quote and estimated delivery time before paying, and only use sellers who seem set up for it regularly rather than a one-off arrangement.
